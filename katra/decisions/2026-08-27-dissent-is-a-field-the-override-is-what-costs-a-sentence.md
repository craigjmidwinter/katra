The ruling makes dissent cheap on purpose and the override expensive: dissent rides the routine reply as a field with a typed null; an override costs a sentence. Whatever is expensive will not happen.

That shape is **not built yet**, and this records why, so whoever builds it does not reinvent the reasoning.

**When it lands, it is a field on the task, never a separate document.** A dissent record living beside the thing it dissents from is a second store to keep in sync, and the whole seam exists because two stores half-knowing one fact is how they come to disagree. The same argument that moved `on_deck` into katra applies one level down.

**The asymmetry is the mechanism, not decoration.** A field that is normally absent costs nothing to fill, so a Manager who disagrees records it in passing. An override requires a reason string, so a Director who overrides has to say why — and the cost lands on the party with the authority rather than on the party being overruled. Reverse the asymmetry and the pushback Craig said is *expected* becomes the thing that takes effort, which means it stops happening and the ruling quietly inverts.

**Not built now because the surface it attaches to does not exist yet.** Ranking lives in `on_deck` in nine plan files today; those migrate into katra tasks by each project's own session, and that is brainstem's half plus each project's work — explicitly not a sweep from here. A dissent field on a ranking katra does not yet hold would be a field with nothing to disagree about.

**What is already true and does not wait:** `author` records who ranked, `claimed_by` records who took it up, and both are opaque. When dissent lands it should be the third of that family — a fact about a task, recorded on the task, interpreted by whoever reads it.
