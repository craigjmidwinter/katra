The problem stated as a fact rather than a hope. getvect's devlog is public at getvect.midwinter.io/devlog, generated by katra build from site/devlog/data.json, and nothing on that page says what produced it. A reader who likes the stamped entries and the decision records has no way to learn that the thing they like is a tool they could use. Every published katra is a shopfront with no sign on it.

Placement was the one real design call, and the obvious answer was wrong. The viewer already has a .kv-side-foot at the bottom of the sidebar, which looks like the natural home for a credit. styles.css hides .kv-side entirely below 860px — so a colophon there is invisible to every phone reader, which is a large share of anyone arriving from a shared link. Verified in the browser rather than reasoned about: at the mobile breakpoint the sidebar foot measures zero width while the colophon still renders. It goes at the end of .kv-main, the scrolling column every view builds, which is also one insertion in route() instead of edits to seven view functions.

Wording: 'Chronicled with katra', not 'Built with' or 'Powered by'. A discovery line has one job, which is to make a stranger click, and 'Built with katra' teaches them a name while 'Chronicled with katra' teaches them a name and a category. The category is the part that converts. Only the tool name is a link; no icon, no version, no second line. It is a colophon in the printer's sense, and it has to earn its place on someone else's published page.

Default on, genuinely removable. colophon: false in katra/config.yml, honoured on the next build, no justification required — documented under both 'colophon' and the word 'footer', because someone turning it off will search for what they saw rather than what it is called. Config.Colophon is a *bool so an explicit false is distinguishable from an absent key, matching the memory.enabled idiom rather than inventing a second one. Both behaviours are tested and both tests were faulted: forcing the credit always-on fails the suppressible test, defaulting it off fails the other.
